Research date for all entries below: **2026-06-11**. One bullet per load-bearing claim, with source title + URL.

## Azure DevOps work-item structure & field facts

- ADO Agile process hierarchy is Epic → Feature → User Story → Task; User Stories and Tasks track work, Epics and Features group work under larger scenarios. — *Define features and epics to organize your backlog* (Microsoft Learn) — https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/devops/boards/backlogs/define-features-epics?view=azure-devops
- A Feature is "a significant piece of functionality that delivers value to the user," typically several user stories, may take one or more sprints. An Epic is "a large body of work that can be broken down into multiple features," may span releases. Backlog items (stories/tasks) should generally complete within a sprint. — *Define features and epics* — https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/devops/boards/backlogs/define-features-epics?view=azure-devops#what-makes-a-feature-or-epic
- For Features/Epics, "enter as much detail as possible to help your team understand the scope, estimate the work, develop tests, and ensure that the product meets acceptance criteria." — *Define features and epics* — https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/devops/boards/backlogs/define-features-epics?view=azure-devops#add-details-to-a-feature-or-epic
- Value Area field: Architectural (technical services to implement business features) or Business (default; services that directly deliver customer value). Optional Feature/Story estimation fields: Story Points / Effort / Size, Priority, Risk, Business Value. — *Agile workflow in Azure Boards* and *Define features and epics* — https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/devops/boards/work-items/guidance/agile-process-workflow?view=azure-devops#define-user-stories
- User Story Description guidance: "provide enough detail for estimating how much work is required... Focus on who the feature is for, what users want to accomplish, and why. Don't describe how the feature should be developed. Do provide sufficient details so your team can write tasks and test cases." — *Agile workflow in Azure Boards* — https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/devops/boards/work-items/guidance/agile-process-workflow?view=azure-devops#define-user-stories
- Acceptance Criteria field guidance: "Provide the criteria to be met before the bug or user story can be closed. Before work begins, describe the customer acceptance criteria as clearly as possible... You can use the acceptance criteria as the basis for acceptance tests to more effectively evaluate whether an item is satisfactorily completed." Acceptance Criteria field exists on Bug, Epic, Feature, Product backlog item/User Story — NOT on Task. — *Agile workflow in Azure Boards* / *Query by titles, IDs, and rich-text fields* — https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/devops/boards/queries/titles-ids-descriptions?view=azure-devops#common-fields-for-most-work-item-types
- A user story moves to Closed "when the product owner agrees the story is implemented according to the Acceptance Criteria and acceptance tests pass." — *Agile workflow in Azure Boards* — https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/devops/boards/work-items/guidance/agile-process-workflow?view=azure-devops#track-progress
- Title is the only required field for all work item types; max 255 characters; "A short description that summarizes the work item and helps team members distinguish it from others." — *Query by titles, IDs, and rich-text fields* / *About work items* — https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/devops/boards/queries/titles-ids-descriptions?view=azure-devops
- Scrum guidance, equally applicable: "When you define a product backlog item, focus on the customer value and avoid describing implementation details." — *Manage Scrum process work item types & workflow* — https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/devops/boards/work-items/guidance/scrum-process-workflow?view=azure-devops#define-pbis-and-bugs
- Universal ADO best practices: "Write specific, actionable descriptions with clear acceptance criteria"; "Use user stories with clear acceptance criteria"; use consistent estimation. — *Manage work items effectively* — https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/devops/boards/backlogs/manage-work-items?view=azure-devops#work-item-management-best-practices
- ADO best practices for Agile: "Use Feature to represent customer-facing value"; "Use Task for developer work that fits within an iteration." — *Best practices for Agile product management* — https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/devops/boards/best-practices-agile-project-management?view=azure-devops
- Tasks roll up into features; the Description field supports rich text and images and is "a powerful tool for use when specifying work." — *DevOps (cloud-native architecture)* — https://learn.microsoft.com/dotnet/architecture/cloud-native/devops#task-management

## INVEST (origin + each letter)

- INVEST acronym was coined by Bill Wake in his August 17, 2003 article "INVEST in Good Stories, and SMART Tasks." Same article repurposed SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-boxed) for tasks decomposed from stories. — *INVEST in Good Stories, and SMART Tasks* (Bill Wake, XP123) — https://xp123.com/articles/invest-in-good-stories-and-smart-tasks/
- Wake's letter meanings: Independent (mostly independent capabilities); Negotiable (not specifications but solution ideas explored together — a conversation, not a contract); Valuable (valuable to those who care about the product); Estimable (can be estimated in terms of work required); Small (on the scale of hours to days — fits within an iteration); Testable (understood well enough to describe examples and behaviors). — *INVEST in Good Stories, and SMART Tasks* — https://xp123.com/articles/invest-in-good-stories-and-smart-tasks/
- INVEST = Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable; widely adopted, included in Mike Cohn's *User Stories Applied* (2004). — *What does INVEST Stand For?* (Agile Alliance) — https://agilealliance.org/glossary/invest/
- Dependencies between stories cause delays — you don't get working software until all dependent stories are complete (Independent rationale). Story must give enough detail to estimate (Estimable); short iterations require small stories (Small). — *INVEST criteria for Agile user stories* (Boost) — https://www.boost.co.nz/blog/2021/10/invest-criteria/

## Definition of Ready (DoR)

- DoR is a set of criteria a backlog item must meet before it's accepted into a sprint; a quality gate ensuring work is sufficiently detailed, understood, and actionable before development begins. Collaboratively established by product owner and team. — *Definition of Ready (DoR) Explained* (Atlassian) — https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/definition-of-ready
- A DoR commonly checks that the item is clear/described, has well-defined acceptance criteria, is estimated/sized, has dependencies identified, and conforms to INVEST. — *Definition of Ready* (Atlassian) + *Definition of ready for Agile user stories* (Boost) — https://www.boost.co.nz/blog/2022/07/definition-of-ready-agile-user-stories
- Microsoft's own engineering guidance maintains a Definition of Ready for backlog items before they enter a sprint (shared understanding, acceptance criteria, estimable, no blocking dependencies). — *Definition of Ready* (Microsoft Code-With Engineering Playbook) — https://microsoft.github.io/code-with-engineering-playbook/agile-development/team-agreements/definition-of-ready/

## Acceptance criteria: testability & Given/When/Then (Gherkin)

- Gherkin (used by Cucumber/BDD) structures behavior with keywords Given, When, Then, And, But. Given = initial context/state; When = the action or trigger; Then = the observable, expected outcome. — *Gherkin Reference* (Cucumber) — https://cucumber.io/docs/gherkin/reference/
- A scenario represents a single behavior; chaining multiple When/Then in one scenario signals it should be split into multiple scenarios. Background defines steps common to all scenarios. — *Gherkin Reference* (Cucumber) — https://cucumber.io/docs/gherkin/reference/
- Two common AC formats: scenario-oriented (Given/When/Then) and rule-oriented (checklist). Given/When/Then provides context and maps directly to test cases; checklists are simpler for straightforward requirements but lack context for complex behavior. — *Acceptance Criteria: Purposes, Formats and Best Practices* (AltexSoft) — https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/acceptance-criteria-purposes-formats-and-best-practices/
- Acceptance criteria must be independently testable with clear pass/fail outcomes; avoid vague terms and prefer measurable outcomes (e.g. "loads within 2 seconds" not "loads quickly"). "If you can't test it, it's not done." — *Acceptance Criteria* (AltexSoft) + *Given-When-Then Acceptance Criteria* (Parallel) — https://www.parallelhq.com/blog/given-when-then-acceptance-criteria

## Titles & descriptions

- Title should summarize the item and distinguish it from others; keep within 255 chars. — *Query by titles, IDs, and rich-text fields* (Microsoft Learn) — https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/devops/boards/queries/titles-ids-descriptions?view=azure-devops
- The classic user-story user-voice template "As a <type of user>, I want <goal>, so that <reason/benefit>" frames who/what/why and is the conventional story form. — *What does INVEST Stand For?* (Agile Alliance) + *Acceptance Criteria* (AltexSoft) — https://agilealliance.org/glossary/invest/
