# INVEST for User Stories

## Summary
INVEST is a six-point checklist for judging whether a User Story is well-formed: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable. The acronym was coined by Bill Wake in his 2003 article "INVEST in Good Stories, and SMART Tasks" and is widely adopted as shorthand for story quality (per Bill Wake / XP123; Agile Alliance). A story that satisfies all six is generally ready to estimate and pull into a sprint; a story that fails one points to a specific weakness to fix. INVEST applies primarily to User Stories — the team-scoped, sprint-sized requirement level — and often forms the backbone of a team's Definition of Ready.

**Also known as:** INVEST, INVEST criteria, INVEST model, good story checklist, story quality, independent negotiable valuable estimable small testable, Bill Wake INVEST.

## Origin
Bill Wake introduced the INVEST acronym in his article "INVEST in Good Stories, and SMART Tasks," posted on 17 August 2003 (per Bill Wake / XP123). The same article repurposed SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-boxed) for the tasks that result from decomposing a story. INVEST was later popularized through Mike Cohn's *User Stories Applied* (2004) and is now a standard reference for story quality (per Agile Alliance: What does INVEST Stand For?). In Extreme Programming, a story has three parts — Card, Conversation, Confirmation — and INVEST describes the qualities a good story-on-a-card should have (per Bill Wake / XP123).

## The Six Criteria
Each criterion below carries Wake's intent and a one-line check you can apply to a story.

### Independent
Stories should be mostly self-contained capabilities, not chained to other stories (per Bill Wake / XP123). Dependencies cause delays, because dependent work delivers no usable software until every linked story is complete (per Boost: INVEST criteria). Independence also lets stories be reordered and prioritized freely.
- **Check:** Can this story be built and delivered without waiting on another unfinished story?

### Negotiable
A story is a solution idea explored together, not a fixed specification or contract. It captures intent and leaves room for the team and product owner to shape the "how" through conversation (per Bill Wake / XP123). This is why a story's Description should focus on who/what/why rather than prescribing implementation.
- **Check:** Does the story state the need while leaving the solution open to discussion, rather than dictating a design?

### Valuable
A story must be valuable to those who care about the product — the users or stakeholders (per Bill Wake / XP123). The value should be explicit, which is what the user-voice "so that …" clause captures. Work with no stated value is hard to prioritize and may not belong on the backlog.
- **Check:** Can you name the user or stakeholder benefit this story delivers?

### Estimable
The team must be able to estimate the work a story requires, at least approximately (per Bill Wake / XP123). A story that cannot be estimated usually hides an unanswered question, an unfamiliar technology, or excessive size — each a signal to clarify or split (per Boost: INVEST criteria). Estimability is what makes sprint planning and forecasting possible.
- **Check:** Does the team have enough understanding to put a size on it?

### Small
A good story is on the scale of hours to days and fits within a single iteration (per Bill Wake / XP123). Short iterations require small stories so the team can deliver working software and get fast feedback each sprint (per Boost: INVEST criteria). A story too large to finish in a sprint should be split. In Azure DevOps terms, stories should generally complete within a sprint, while Features and Epics span more (per Microsoft Learn: Define features and epics).
- **Check:** Can this be completed within a single sprint?

### Testable
A story must be understood well enough to describe concrete examples and expected behaviors — that is, to write tests for it (per Bill Wake / XP123). Testability is the bridge to acceptance criteria: a story you cannot test is a story whose "done" no one can agree on. In Azure DevOps, a story is closed only when it meets its Acceptance Criteria and acceptance tests pass (per Microsoft Learn: Agile workflow in Azure Boards).
- **Check:** Can you state at least one concrete pass/fail example of the story working?

## Using INVEST
INVEST is a diagnostic, not a scorecard. When a story feels off, walking the six letters usually pinpoints why: a story that cannot be estimated is often not small enough or not understood enough to be testable; a story with no clear value may be a task in disguise; a story that cannot be made independent may need re-splitting along a different seam. Many teams fold INVEST directly into their Definition of Ready, treating "passes INVEST" as part of the bar a story clears before sprint planning (per Boost: Definition of ready for Agile user stories).

INVEST is tuned for User Stories. Features and Epics are larger by design — they are not meant to be Small or fully Independent — so they are judged on different criteria (clear value, decomposability, framed scope). Tasks, being the technical decomposition of a story, are better evaluated with SMART than INVEST.
